## Working title

**Regional constraint and developmental isoform specialization in mammalian
DNMT3A**

## Central question

How did mammalian DNMT3A partition sequence constraint and developmental
methylation function between the DNMT3A1-specific regulatory tail and the
shorter DNMT3A2-like architecture?

## Central result

The DNMT3A1 N terminus is not uniformly fast-evolving. Residues 1–163 contain
most of its mammalian amino-acid diversity, whereas the experimentally
supported nucleosome/H2AK119ub-engagement region at residues 164–219 is strongly
conserved. Only 6 of 56 retained sites in the engagement region are
polymorphic, compared with 132 of 154 sites upstream
(`p = 3.26e-24`). Recurrent variability shows the same contrast: 5 of 56 sites
versus 107 of 154 (`p = 6.83e-16`).

The regional result is connected to isoform biology at two independent
levels. Downstream-start-compatible products occur across placental and
marsupial orders, consistent with a therian-ancestral architecture. Mouse
perturbation data then support a developmental shift from a larger DNMT3A2
methylation contribution at E15.5 to DNMT3A1 dominance in P21 neuronal mCA.

## Supporting results

1. The PWWP, ADD, and catalytic domains show strong mammalian constraint.
2. The upstream DNMT3A1-specific disordered tail is the principal reservoir of
   coding variation.
3. T12, G34, and S97 have reproducible lineage trajectories and are suitable
   hypotheses, but none is a corrected functional discovery. Neither a
   disordered-tail property screen nor two ESM-2 models identify the tier-1
   substitutions as exceptional relative to recurrent natural alternatives.
4. DNMT3A1/DNMT3A2 transcript architecture and developmental expression provide
   isoform context, but do not connect the coding candidates to brain
   phenotypes.
5. All-context reanalysis of eight P21 neuronal methylomes shows that
   `Dnmt3a1` loss nearly eliminates mCA above matched lambda background,
   `Dnmt3a2` loss retains bulk mCA, and deletion of the DNMT3A1 N terminus
   retains about 40% of WT mCA. This experimentally links the conserved
   isoform-specific region to neuronal methylation, but not any individual
   evolutionary substitution.
6. Matched whole-cortex expression changes are only very weakly associated
   with gene-body mCA changes, placing a quantitative limit on a broad
   transcriptional-effect claim.
7. Independently defined MeCP2-repressed neuronal genes carry unusually high
   WT mCA and are strongly enriched among the largest absolute
   DNMT3A1-dependent losses. Fractional-retention sensitivity tests do not
   support preferential targeting of these genes by the DNMT3A1 N terminus.
8. Source-study cortex chromatin integration provides locus-level mechanistic
   context. WT DNMT3A occupancy strongly predicts mCA preservation after
   DNMT3A2 loss. After DNMT3A1 N-terminal deletion, H3K27me3-rich gene bodies
   show modestly greater mCA loss even when WT DNMT3A occupancy and baseline
   covariates are modeled together. This is a descriptive Polycomb-context
   association, not evidence of direct H3K27me3 recognition.
9. A directly matched P21 neuronal H2AK119ub test narrows the interpretation.
   H2AK119ub alone predicts greater mCA loss after N-tail deletion, but its
   coefficient disappears after joint adjustment for H3K27me3. H3K27me3 also
   predicts relative DNMT3A occupancy loss, yet occupancy change does not
   predict mCA change gene by gene. The defensible result is chromatin-context
   dependence, not an established H2AK119ub recruitment pathway.
10. Two independently prepared 500 ps WT–L188H pairs sample different local
    interface geometries and opposite UDR-mobility directions. L188H remains a
    structural hypothesis, but the simulations do not supply a reproducible
    molecular mechanism.

## Claim hierarchy

### Primary claims

- Mammalian DNMT3A contains sharply contrasting evolutionary regimes.
- The known 164–219 engagement region is much more conserved than the upstream
  disordered tail.
- Functional constraint is heterogeneous even within the DNMT3A1-specific
  N terminus.

### Secondary claims

- T12 and G34 are alignment-robust evolutionary candidates with
  `BH q = 0.0871`.
- S97 is an alignment-robust but weaker candidate with `BH q = 0.7206`.
- Recurrent lineage substitutions provide a controlled variant panel for
  molecular experiments.
- DNMT3A1 is the dominant source of bulk P21 neuronal mCA in GSE164265, and
  its N-terminal region contributes substantially to the full phenotype.

### Claims not supported

- The candidate substitutions alter DNMT3A activity or regulation.
- The substitutions were driven by brain evolution or transposable elements.
- Candidate state predicts neuronal mCH or developmental expression.
- Any candidate is specific to humans or explains human CH methylation.

The promoter section can now contrast two regulatory architectures. Human and
mouse DNMT3A1 use syntenically corresponding clusters of full-length-
transcript TSSs, whereas the DNMT3A2 analysis supports a distinct internal
promoter. Cross-therian ancestry remains better supported for transcript
architecture than for exact promoter sequence.

Expanded sampling identifies downstream-start-compatible products in 13 of 18
placental orders and in two separated marsupial orders. This distribution is
compatible with a therian-ancestral internal-transcript architecture.
Detection remains strongly annotation-dependent, so lineage losses cannot be
called. A strict great-ape-consensus screen finds no enrichment of fixed
human-branch substitutions in either DNMT3A promoter relative to local aligned
flanks. The manuscript should therefore make a mammalian regulatory-remodeling
claim, not a human-specific promoter-evolution claim.

## Decision rule for further work

Additional post hoc selection analyses on the same alignments should stop.
Further work should add one of three independent evidence types:

1. a molecular phenotype for a prespecified variant;
2. comparable methylome or expression measurements from non-reference
   lineages; or
3. direct promoter/TSS evidence that resolves isoform evolution.

Without new evidence of one of these types, T12, G34, and S97 remain supporting
hypotheses rather than the manuscript's organizing result.

The completed property and protein-language-model screens do not supply that
missing evidence. Their value is to prevent overinterpretation and to support
the regional-constraint result as the manuscript's organizing result.
